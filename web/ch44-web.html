Lecture 2 — The Rayleigh Law (the mathematics of throwing water away)

The register's first formal isotope-separation mathematics face, and this lecture's source. Part 5 of the program (N139) carries two chapter titles that are the campaign's hinge: "Deuterium math introduction" and "Rayleigh equation analysis" [frozen got-strings] — the performer teaching himself, on camera and at watch-page level (the partial-data trio's honest gap, printed at Lecture 3), the law the whole program lives by. The book states the law the way it states every law: derived, then verified twice.

The derivation, discrete first. A reservoir of water, volume V₀, D/H ratio R₀ (the abundance itself, 155.76 ppm), under electrolysis. The cathode consumes hydrogen and deuterium at rates whose ratio is the separation factor S — hydrogen preferentially, S > 1. Slice off a small volume dV as gas: the deuterium left behind concentrates slightly, the hydrogen leaves faster than its share. Per slice, the deuterium ledger is dR/R = (1/S − 1)·dV/V (deuterium lost at rate 1/S of its share while volume shrinks at rate dV/V), and integrating the slices from V₀ down to V gives the closed form: R/R₀ = f^(1/S−1), with f = V/V₀ the volume fraction remaining. The enrichment factor the whole campaign bookkeeps: E(f, S) = f^(1/S−1). The form's honesty is the lesson: the exponent (1/S − 1) is small — one ninth at the premise's S = 10 — so the enrichment grows as a fractional power of the volume thrown away: to gain a factor of E you throw away roughly E^(S/(S−1)) of your water. Separation by dilution: the mathematics of throwing water away, exactly as the campaign's subtitle says.

The two-method verification, the engine's own. The Rayleigh rows are the reason Engine 63 has a transcendental lane, and the lab verifies every one of them two ways [Sheet 3's E-rows]: the closed form E = f^(1/S−1) computed as exp(p·ln f) on exact rational exponents (−9/10 for the premise's S = 10, −5/6 for the industrial S = 6), against an 8-stage staged product — the reservoir drained in eight equal stages, the per-stage factor applied multiplicatively, the staged product reassembled limb by limb.
