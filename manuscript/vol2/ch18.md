# Vol II · Chapter 18 — The Halogens and Their Salts: One Family, the Whole Ladder, Three Shelves

## Lecture 0 — The family the household sells only in its safe states, read before anything is opened

Chapter 15 climbed the strong-acid ladder; Chapter 16 walked the oxidizer shelf and priced its demand; Chapter 17 found one element holding its whole oxidation ladder on four shelves. This chapter walks the one *family* the E° meter reads cleanest — the halogens, the column that runs from the strongest oxidizer the periodic table sells to the gentlest one the bench can titrate — and it opens with the observation that makes the family a chapter rather than a table: **the household sells the halogens only in their *spent* states.** The salt box sells chloride. The spa shelf sells bromide. The medicine cabinet sells iodide and its element. The toothpaste tube sells fluoride. Every halogen the world sells an ordinary person is the −1 face — the bottom rung, the state with no more electrons to give — and every dangerous face of the family (chlorine the war gas, bromine the burn, fluorine the never) is a *step up* the ladder that no shelf takes for you, that the bench can take only by choice, and that this book prices at the meter before it schedules, and schedules only twice.

**The thesis, and it is the volume's hinge stated a fourth time: the halogen family is the oxidizer scale sold by the gram as salts — the −1 faces are the *products* the world packages, and the elements are the *work* the bench must do itself — and the discipline this chapter teaches is reading which rung a label holds, stepping the family up only by the two doors whose safety architecture exists (the dilute bromine-water door and the iodine door, both at sink scale, both trapped), letting the trades take the other steps for you (the spa's bank, the pool's puck, the jug's census — chlorine as *products*, never as gas), and never once generating the faces the book refuses to make.**

Sit at the kitchen table with the notebook and pour nothing. The pencil-and-paper census, before any apparatus:

- **The kitchen shelf** — the salt box: sodium chloride, the family's chloride face by the pound, and the water softener's potassium chloride beside it — the cheapest halide bank in the house, and the brine cell's own feedstock (Chapter 12 built it).
- **The pool shelf** — the chlorine products of Chapter 16's census: the jug (hypochlorite), the granular shock (calcium hypochlorite), the TCCA puck — the household's *only* chlorine faces, all sold as salts and dosed into water, never as the element.
- **The spa shelf** — the bottle labeled *bromine base* or *sodium bromide* (the trade sells the bromide bank by the pound), the BCDMH tablets (a mixed halogen releaser, ≥60% available halogen), and the non-chlorine shock bucket that *activates* the bank — the spa's entire chemistry is the −1 face plus an oxidizer, run at ppm.
- **The medicine cabinet** — the iodine shelf: the tincture bottle (USP: 2% iodine, 2.4% sodium iodide, 47% alcohol), the povidone-iodine (10% w/v product, 1% available iodine), and the Lugol's solution behind the pharmacist's counter (5 g iodine + 10 g potassium iodide per 100 mL) — the family's element sold in *banked* form: the iodide keeps the iodine dissolved, and the label is the triiodide equilibrium in a brown bottle.
- **The bathroom shelf** — the toothpaste: sodium fluoride or stannous fluoride at the thousand-ppm class — the fluorine face, sold in the only form this chapter will ever print for it. Recognition only, from here to the end of the book.
- **The photo-trade heritage** — silver nitrate, the one halide-testing reagent the census owns but the shelf rarely sells anymore: the most expensive gram on the bench, and the trade that built itself on silver bromide's light sensitivity (Chapter 32's inheritance, cited here where the family's test lives).

That is four faces on six shelves, and the census's quiet observation is the thesis seen in the packaging: **not one shelf sells a halogen element at a concentration this book would generate.** The salt, the puck, the bank, the tincture, the toothpaste — all −1 (or the element dissolved in its own iodide at the pharmacy's antiseptic scale); the elements themselves — Cl₂ a green gas that boils at −34 °C [convergent], Br₂ a red-brown liquid that evaporates at room temperature (bp 58.8 °C), I₂ a violet solid that sublimes in a closed bottle — are the faces this chapter *prices*, generates exactly twice at sink scale, and otherwise prints through Chapter 28's recognition lens. The chapter's five questions, in bench order: **what does each label's number mean (the assay conventions of the antiseptic trade, the spa bank, and the pool shelf, translated), which rung does the meter say I hold (the family ladder from +2.87 to +0.535), what is the household already doing with the family (the spa's bank-and-activate cycle, the pool's chlorine-as-salt discipline — N77's generator priced as the boundary it declares itself to be), what are the bench's two honest doors (the bromine-water door of N62 and the iodine door of N08, both corpus-built, both scheduled), and how does the family close the volume's assay chain (the iodometric titrant family — the primary standard, the iodine number, and the jug's own assay)?**

One boundary before any apparatus is unpacked, and it is the book's binding line for this entire chapter, inherited from Chapters 15/16/17 and stated here at its strictest, because this family holds the most acutely toxic substances the volume has benched: **the chlorine face is never generated by any procedure in this book.** The three doors to it — the lab tradition's manganese dioxide and hydrochloric acid, the pool puck and muriatic acid (N77's generator, the register's own do-not-attempt face: the channel's own words, "chlorine gas is highly toxic and full PPE and a gas mask must be worn; this should not be attempted at home under any circumstances"), and the brine cell run for its gas instead of its alkali — are printed as *recognition equations*, priced at the meter and at the flask, and never opened. **The bromine face is benched only as dilute bromine water, at sink scale, outdoors, gloved** (N62's own door — and the honest-result datum that made it the register's safety-culture exemplar); the distillation route to the liquid element (N54) is printed at recognition level, its 58.8 °C condenser and its iced flask priced but not scheduled. **The iodine face is benched** — the family's least violent member, still a corrosive solid whose violet vapor is its own warning label, still match-scale, still ventilated. **And the fluorine face is recognition-only, full stop: no compositions, no procedures, no equations beyond the meter's one row.** The element the E° table prices at +2.87 V is the one this book will never help anyone reach; the hydrofluoric-acid hazard class it implies (the glass-etching, deep-penetrating burn the medical literature treats as its own emergency) is Chapter 28's to teach at depth, and this chapter hands it the one row and the closed door.

## Lecture 1 — The halogen census: labels written by four trades that never say "halogen"

**How you could have found it.** Buy one item from each shelf, read the label, and try to answer the only question that matters at the bench: *how many moles am I holding, and at which rung?* The halogen shelf answers in four mutually incompatible conventions — and two of them the previous chapters already built, which is the volume's own economy: the pool shelf's *available chlorine* is Chapter 16's convention carried; the antiseptic trade's *w/v available iodine* is its pharmacy cousin; the spa shelf's *bank* is a new one; and the toothpaste's *ppm* is the dilution ladder of Chapter 4 run to four decimal places.

**Convention one: weight percent of the element, the antiseptic trade.** The USP tincture label is the census's cleanest translation: **2% iodine, 2.4% sodium iodide, 47% alcohol** [convergent — the current retail label class, e.g. the CVS tincture's own Drug Facts panel] — and the two numbers on one label are the chapter's first lesson in halogen banking: the elemental iodine (0.10 g in the 5 mL bottle) is *held dissolved* by an iodide bank more than twice its size in moles (0.801 mmol of iodide against 0.394 mmol of iodine — Lab 18.1, Run 5), because iodine alone barely touches water (0.03 g/100 mL, the honest 1.2 mM) and iodine plus iodide is the triiodide solution, the brown bottle's actual chemistry. Lugol's solution runs the same bank five times denser (5 g iodine + 10 g potassium iodide per 100 mL — the FDA's own BAM formula, 50 mg/mL of iodine [convergent]) and the povidone bottle runs the *available-iodine* convention at its most generous label: a 10% w/v product that is 1% available iodine [convergent] — the iodophor's polymer is the bank, and the label says so in the only honest way the trade has: the element is one-tenth of the product.

**Convention two: the bank, the spa trade.** The bottle labeled *bromine base* is sodium bromide at the hardware-store grade — N62's own sourcing face, "1-2 lb bottles at $4-5/lb," the register's cross-channel datum (N54's pool/spa KBr is the same shelf, different cation) — and the trade's label hides the assay behind a *practice*: build a "bromide bank" in the water, then *activate* it with an oxidizer (the non-chlorine shock bucket, or the hypochlorite the tablets carry). The census translation is one honest division (Lab 18.1, Run 5): sodium bromide is **77.66% bromine by mass** — N62's own planning figure, reproduced by the engine to the digit — and a 454 g (1 lb) bottle is a **4.412-mol bromide bank**, priced recognition-only in full (Lab 18.1's Run 5 line: the whole bottle *could* ceiling at 352.6 g of bromine; the chlorine demand for that ceiling is 312.9 g — 107.9 L of gas at RTP; the trade never runs this arithmetic because the trade doses *ppm*, which is the point). The BCDMH tablets carry the trade's other convention, *available halogen* (≥60% class [convergent]) — available-chlorine's mixed-halogen cousin, Chapter 16's arithmetic extended one element over.

**Convention three: available chlorine, carried.** The pool shelf needs no new translation — Chapter 16's Run 5 built it, and the census re-pins it (Lab 18.1, Run 3): the jug at 5.25% sodium hypochlorite is 5.00% available chlorine (×0.95250); the puck at ~90% available chlorine; the granular shock's 65–70% as product assay against the pure salt's 99.18% ceiling. What the census *adds* is the family reading: the pool shelf sells the family's +1 and +5 faces in mineral form, and the gas between them (the Cl₂ the hypochlorite and the acid would make) is exactly the face the binding line refuses — the pool trade's own discipline, dose-into-water-never-generate, is the binding line read as a business model.

**Convention four: ppm, the water-treatment trade.** The toothpaste's thousand-ppm fluoride and the camping chapter's iodine-drops-per-liter (the wilderness-medicine class: ~5 drops of 2% tincture per liter of clear water, 30 minutes [convergent]) are the dilution ladder of Chapter 4 run at its extremes — and both are dosing conventions, not assay conventions: they assume a concentration and tell you a *use*. The bench's translation is the titration (Lecture 5), and the camping class prices it: ~5 drops of tincture per liter is roughly 5–8 mg of iodine — about 0.02–0.03 mmol — dosed to kill what grows in a liter of water; the toothpaste's 1000 ppm is 1 mg per gram of paste; and the difference between a *dose* and an *assay* is the whole discipline of the chapter's second half.

**The census itself, in the bench's currency** (Lab 18.1, Run 5, executed verbatim below): the tincture's 0.394 mmol of iodine per bottle; Lugol's 0.1970 M iodine riding a 0.6024 M iodide bank (3.06× the element — the bank is the solvent); the povidone's 1% available; the spa bottle's 77.66% and 4.412-mol bank; the salt box's chloride at the softener grade; the AgX ladder's six orders of magnitude in one family (AgCl 1.8×10⁻¹⁰, white; AgBr 5.0×10⁻¹³, pale yellow; AgI 8.3×10⁻¹⁷, yellow [convergent] — the halide test's colors *are* the solubility rungs, Chapter 9's rules read as a paint chart).

Three census lessons, each load-bearing. **First, the family's safe states are the cheap states.** Chloride is table salt; bromide is a pool additive; iodide is an antiseptic's bank; fluoride is toothpaste — the −1 faces are sold by the pound, the elements by the gram if at all, and the price ladder *is* the reactivity ladder read upside down. **Second, every element in the house is already banked.** The tincture's iodine dissolves because its iodide holds it; the spa's bromine *is* its bromide bank waiting for an oxidizer; the pool's chlorine is hypochlorite waiting for nothing — and the bench that wants an element face reads the label for the bank, not for the element. **Third, the family closes the assay chain.** Chapter 13 standardized permanganate; Chapter 17 pinned thiosulfate with it; this chapter adds the family's own primary standard (iodate), the titration the whole analytical tradition runs on iodine for, and the assay that reads the household's biggest halogen holding (the jug) — the iodometric titrant family the volume promised at Chapter 17's close, delivered in Lecture 5.

## Lecture 2 — The meter on the family: the ladder read as one column

Chapter 12 priced the electron on one couple; Chapter 16 read the shelf's demand; Chapter 17 read one element's ladder. The halogen bench reads the *family* — and the family is the E° table's cleanest read in the whole book, because the halogens are the one group whose elemental couples line up monotonically with the periodic table's own order. All values acid-standard and web-verified this session at the [convergent] class unless labeled otherwise, all consistent with the couples Chapters 11/12/16/17 pinned:

| Couple (reduction) | E° (V) | | Couple (reduction) | E° (V) |

| **F₂/F⁻** | **+2.87** (recognition-only — the strongest common oxidizer) | | **Br₂(aq)/Br⁻** | **+1.087** (the liquid-element variant +1.066 named) |

Read the table top to bottom and the family's whole behavior falls out of the one subtraction. **The displacement ladder is the meter's own arithmetic:** chlorine sits +0.271 V above bromine and +0.823 V above iodine, so chlorine displaces both — the reaction the seawater bromine industry runs at world scale (Cl₂ + 2 NaBr → 2 NaCl + Br₂, BALANCED) and the reaction the bench runs as its gentlest halogen generation (Cl₂ + 2 KI → 2 KCl + I₂); bromine sits +0.552 V above iodine, so bromine water displaces iodide — the unsaturation test's own pre-chemistry. **And the sign runs one way only:** iodine over the bromide couple prices at −0.552 V, +106.5 kJ uphill — *no* halogen lower in the family displaces one higher; the ladder's direction is the family's own arrow, and the household's classic demonstration (the ordered test tubes, chlorine in each, the colors migrating down the family) is this one subtraction shown as a rainbow.

The lecture's one idea, and it deserves its own paragraph. **The halogens are the only family whose oxidizer strength the periodic table orders by position — and the household's packaging of the family follows the same order read backwards.** Fluorine at +2.87 V is the family's unreachable face (the recognition row); chlorine at +1.358 is sold only as its own reduced products (the pool shelf's salts — the trade will not bottle the gas for a household, and neither will this book); bromine at +1.087 is sold as the *bank* (the spa's bromide, waiting for an activator the trade also sells); iodine at +0.535 is sold as the *element* itself, banked in iodide, on the antiseptic shelf — the only halogen element an ordinary person can buy at a pharmacy, and exactly the one the family's ladder makes safest. **The shelf's packaging is the meter's own safety architecture, expressed as retail:** the higher the couple, the more completely the trade pre-reduces the element before it will sell it.
