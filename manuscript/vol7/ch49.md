## Lecture 1 — The Photochrom (one proton, one wavelength, one solid)

**The molecule and its two forms.** DNBP, 2-(2,4-dinitrobenzyl)pyridine, C₁₂H₉N₃O₄, 259.221 g/mol [computed; NEW] — a colorless-to-brownish-yellow crystalline solid, the feed's benzyl carbon carrying two nitro groups, one of them ortho and pointed at the pyridine nitrogen like a drawn bow. That geometry is the whole trick. Under ultraviolet light — the episode's 400 nm figure [register] — a proton hops from the benzylic carbon, through the ortho-nitro group's oxygen, to the pyridine nitrogen, and the molecule that results is a different tautomer with a different absorption: blue. The episode's mechanism lecture is the register's own physics face: the UV-induced H-transfer to "the NH blue form", a form "this one's much longer lived and can last for hours" [register]. The convergent battery this session confirms every claim at the literature's level: the photochromism fires at 400 nm or less, is driven by two-photon nitro-assisted proton transfer (NAPT) between the ortho-nitro oxygen and the pyridine nitrogen, and the blue carrier is the long-lived enamine (NH) tautomer — its lowest absorption band measured near 660 nm, the blue form's own face, by transient spectroscopy [convergent]. The episode's complementary-color lecture prices the same pair from the other side: a compound that absorbs near 600 nm looks blue because blue is what it refuses to keep [register].

**Why the solid, and why hours.** The register's sharpest materials lesson in this episode is the state dependence: in solution the revert is fast — in acetone the molecule's parts move freely and the blue dies quickly — but in the rigid crystal the tautomer is locked in geometry, and the blue "can last for hours" [register]. The crystal-form dependence goes further, and the episode states it as a sourcing warning: "not all forms of DNBP are necessarily photochromic and it can depend on what solvent it is crystallized from... some commercial sources of it don't exhibit photochromism until they're recrystallized from an appropriate solvent" [register]. The convergent literature agrees and sharpens it: the hydrogen-bond-constrained o-nitro group in a protonated or otherwise locked crystal obviates the photochromism, and the para-nitro isomer — same formula, wrong geometry — does not do the trick at all [convergent]. A photochrom is not a molecule; it is a molecule in the right lattice.
