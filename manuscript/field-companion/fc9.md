## Lecture 2 — Levigation: the trench and the jar

**Stokes' law, the prospecting tool.** Why did the jar sort itself by morning? The pins are Chapter 5's own register, re-grepped in its owner's page: **Δρ 1652 kg/m³ (quartz 2650 against water's 998), g 9.81 m/s², η 1.002e-3 Pa·s [register: Ch 5's Stokes pins]** — and the battery re-derives the jar's whole table at full precision for a 0.1 m column [computed, B1]:

> **sand d = 5e-4 m: v 2.246e-1 m/s, t 0.4451652 s**
> **silt d = 5e-5 m: v 2.246e-3 m/s, t 44.51652 s**
> **clay d = 1e-6 m: v 8.985e-7 m/s, t 111291.3 s = 30.9142 h**

with the checks that make it a ledger rather than a table: **v × t = 0.1 m EXACT all three**, and the time ratios **silt/sand = 100, clay/silt = 2500 EXACT** — the square law audited at its own ratios: tenfold finer, a hundredfold slower; fiftyfold finer, twenty-five-hundredfold slower. **Levigation, the trench version.** The miner's word for the same physics is older than the law: let the water flow, and the stream does the sieving — "the rocks and sand are left behind in the trench" [register: the levigation corpus line, printed at Ch 5, honored here]. ## Lecture 5 — The kiln: temperature as the reagent
