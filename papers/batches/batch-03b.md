### N18. Lime — Primitive Technology (2018-03-06); secondary: SkillCult (2016-01-22)
- Verified URLs: Primitive Technology blog post (author's own, embedding the official video link https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ek3aeUhHaFY): https://primitivetechnology.wordpress.com/2018/03/06/lime/; SkillCult blog (2016-01-22) https://skillcult.com/blog/2016/1/22/burning-shell-lime-in-a-primitive-strawclay-kiln; SkillCult video "PRIMITIVE FIRE SCIENCE! Burning Shells Into Lime In a Grass Straw & Clay Furnace" https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e54ISzGasdo — channel attribution RESOLVED this session (was flagged unverified): the video, the SkillCult blog post, and the Instructables project (https://www.instructables.com/Burn-Seashell-Lime-in-a-Primitive-StrawClay-Kiln/) are one author's "The Pet" straw/clay kiln project; r/PrimitiveTechnology temperature discussion (decomposition needs ~800-900 C; ordinary cooking ovens insufficient) https://www.reddit.com/r/PrimitiveTechnology/comments/knc3nx/.
- Verification level: content-verified (author blog + official video link pinned; secondary-source attribution resolved).
- Faithful summary: shells/limestone (CaCO3) heated above ~840 C in a reconfigured updraft pottery kiln (SkillCult: a straw/clay kiln) calcine to quicklime (CaO) + CO2; water slakes the quicklime to calcium hydroxide (lime putty); the putty re-carbonates over days, hardening into mortar — the FULL lime cycle: carbonate -> oxide -> hydroxide -> carbonate. Process-failure datum: over-burnt lime ("dead lime", extreme heat) will not slake — temperature control is a real variable, not a formality.
- Decisive moves: raw-material sourcing — backyard minerals (shells, limestone, coral) as the chemical inventory (a nature face of P1) [P1]; thermal decomposition — the P7 archetype: carbonate -> oxide [P7]; slaking and re-carbonation close a reversible P7-family cycle [P7]; improvised furnace — pottery kiln reconfigured / straw+clay kiln [P11]; end-use performance as verification — the mortar's setting IS the identity check on the whole cycle [P9].
