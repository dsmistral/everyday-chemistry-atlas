## N65 — The Home Scientist "008 - Forensic Fingerprinting II: Ninhydrin" (fF_v-MymoOk, 2010-01-20)
- Verification: watch page (56,029 views, Jan 20, 2010) + filmot auto-caption transcript. Transcript-level.
- Content: the ninhydrin face — the second of six fingerprinting segments (after N61's iodine fuming). History: for the first 60 years after forensic fingerprinting began only three methods existed (dusting, iodine fuming, silver nitrate); in 1954 ninhydrin was added — a chemical that reacts with the AMINO ACIDS present in fingerprint residues to produce an intense dye (Ruhemann's purple; the auto-captions garble it "rumens purple"). Method: ninhydrin solution sprayed onto paper or other porous material; latent prints develop gradually as purple stains; development accelerated by heat + moisture. Sourcing: real forensic technicians use disposable spray cans; Thompson uses a Walgreens fingertip sprayer ("three for a buck") + the Maker Shed forensic kit (FO-RKD); the own-solution formulation is 0.6% w/v ninhydrin in acetone — 0.6 g per 100 mL, or a 1 g bottle dissolved in ~167 mL acetone; ninhydrin sold by the gram from lab suppliers (Maker Shed, HMS Beagle, Elemental Scientific) [P1/P10]. Hazards: the acetone solution is FLAMMABLE (no open flames or ignition sources); stains skin indelibly — gloves, protective clothing, newspaper/plastic sheeting. THE KINETICS DATUM: at room temperature the reaction takes an hour or two to a day or more; RT development gives SHARPER, higher-contrast prints; acceleration = a sandwich of the specimen with a damp paper towel pressed with an ordinary clothing iron — hours-to-days become a minute or two; alternatives: convection oven / microwave / kitchen oven at high heat + humidity for several minutes [P9 + P11]; low-contrast prints retreated with ninhydrin solution or plain solvent [P9].
- Register datum: a 14-years-old comment asks where the SILVER-NITRATE segment (lab session 8.4) video is — it was never published (the Make: book covers methods the video series does not; the episode-005-class absence corroborated).
- P-faces: P1, P9, P10, P11. Misses: P2-P8, P12 (the 0.6% formulation is reagent prep, not yield bookkeeping — honest boundary call), P13-P16.

## N66 — The Home Scientist "009 - Forensic Fingerprinting III: Superglue" (XgqwMeWlzgI, 2010-01-22)
- Verification: watch page (66,810 views, Jan 22, 2010) + filmot auto-caption transcript. Transcript-level.
- Content: the superglue-fuming face — the primary method for glass and similar NON-porous surfaces (the porous/non-porous complementarity with N61/N65). History: a happy accident in the late 1970s — a Japanese company testing ways to package superglue noticed fingerprints on container surfaces developed as white crystalline traces; word eventually reached an American forensic scientist who developed the standardized procedure [the discovery-by-accident datum]. Apparatus: a sealed container; a small aluminum-foil boat to contain the superglue; a cotton pad ("stolen from my wife's makeup supplies") or cotton ball; damp paper toweling for humidity [P11]. Chemistry: cyanoacrylate vapors + humidity develop prints as WHITE traces; in a sealed container with exposed superglue the prints develop NATURALLY within ~24 h [kinetics]; CHEMICAL ACCELERATION: a small amount of dilute 0.5 M sodium hydroxide placed on top of the superglue drop -> fuming begins quickly, done in ~5 minutes with per-minute lid-lift monitoring [P9]. The NaOH sourcing datum: purchase separately from the Maker Shed science room or another specialty vendor, OR simply substitute CRYSTAL DRAIN OPENER [P1 — the N57 Rooto/drain-opener lineage recurrence]. Post-development: prints on glass/plastic/metal subsequently liftable with tape, dustable, or treatable with dyes including fluorescent dyes under alternate light sources (for patterned surfaces) [the method-combination datum].
- Register datum: a 13-years-ago comment reports the Maker Shed forensic kit DISCONTINUED (the product-availability face of the N49/N55 sourcing datums — 2010 retail routes age).
- P-faces: P1, P9, P10, P11. Misses: P2-P8, P12-P16 (P13 honest miss: vapor generated in the chamber and consumed on the prints — the iodine-fuming parallel, still not counted as in-situ redox).

## N67 — The Home Scientist "010 - Forensic Fingerprinting IV: Gentian Violet" (vBYYrwo8I6s, 2010-01-24)
- Verification: filmot transcript (15.4K filmot-cache views); the watch page SIGN-WALLED this session (the N46 pattern) — verification carried by filmot + the Make: companion article "Forensics Lab 8.6: Revealing Latent Fingerprints Using Gentian Violet" (makezine.com/laboratory-86-revealing-latent-fing/). Transcript + corroborating-article level.
- Content: the specialized ADHESIVE-surface face — gentian violet is used ONLY to develop latent prints on adhesive surfaces (sticky tape); the method-selection logic: ordinary powder adheres to the adhesive at least as well as to the prints, fuming and chemical development methods yield poor results, specialized powders in a water slurry exist, but gentian violet often gives the best results [P10 method-comparison]. THE DRUGSTORE DATUM: gentian violet is an OTC drugstore item — 1% solution at Walgreens, ~$3 for a one-ounce bottle (9% alcohol, "fine once diluted") [P1 — the same drugstore-substitution line as N61's iodine tincture]; or the Maker Shed kit concentrate (FO-RKD). Quantitative prep: concentrate 1% -> 0.1% working solution, 1 part concentrate : 9 parts water (3 mL + 27 mL = 30 mL, measured with the disposable pipette whose top line = 1 mL — chains N63's calibration) [P9]. Economy: the dilute solution saved and REUSED indefinitely [P10]. Evidence-handling methodology: prints most likely near the ENDS of tape specimens where someone gripped — avoid touching those areas; smooth surfaces (glass/plastic) give higher success; sometimes raisable even from porous surfaces [the forensic-hygiene datum]. Process: spray (messy but often best results) or immerse/float; 1-2 minutes for dye binding, respray periodically as it beads; pour off and RETURN the excess to the bottle; rinse gently with water; retreat if prints insufficiently dark; OVER-treatment -> the whole tape violet, low contrast -> ~1 M hydrochloric acid as a CLEARING agent, used carefully (may clear the prints as well as the background) [P9 process control]. Result: one distinct print with strong ridge detail at the corner where grasped + fragmentary partials ("may actually be useful" — the honest-evidence note).
- Chain-out: the next segment is acidified peroxide for brass surfaces / cartridge cases (-> 011).
- P-faces: P1, P9, P10, P11. Misses: P2-P8, P12-P16.
- Book feedstock: Ch 13 (forensics section) + Ch 2 (the drugstore-chemicals map).
