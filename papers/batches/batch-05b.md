### N30. Setting Up a Home Science Laboratory Part III — Chemicals on the Cheap — Robert Bruce Thompson, Make: (series circa 2009; article date to pin at next pass)
- Verified URL: https://makezine.com/setting-up-a-home-science-lab3/ (verified 2026-10-15 by opening and reading the article; based on material from the 2008 book and the then-unpublished forensics guide)
- Verification level reached: content-verified (full article read)
- Faithful summary: alternative chemical sources beyond lab vendors — drugstore, supermarket, hardware store, home improvement center, auto parts store, lawn and garden store, pottery supply store; purity ranges from USP/FCC (food/consumable grades) down to practical/technical grade, often purifiable by recrystallization and distillation; pottery-supply chemicals (mostly carbonates; technical grade but surprisingly pure — potters will not tolerate impure chemicals) as the premier building-block form, because a carbonate reacting with an acid leaves only water and CO2 as byproducts. Worked examples: CuSO4 (root killer) + NaHCO3 (baking soda) -> CuCO3, then split into copper acetate (vinegar), copper chloride (muriatic acid), copper nitrate (HNO3); muriatic acid + steel wool -> ferrous/ferric chloride; ~$3 barium carbonate (pottery supply) -> ~$50 of barium salts.
- Decisive moves: alt-venue OTC sourcing incl. pottery supply [P1] ; carbonate metathesis hub [P3] ; carbonate + acid = salt + water + CO2 only [P4] ; purification of technical grades by recrystallization/distillation [P2] ; byproduct-clean building blocks + $3 -> $50 economics [P10]
- Candidate new patterns: none
- Honest misses: P5, P6, P7, P8, P9, P11 (proper purchased gear), P12 (economics only, no yield ledger), P13
- Feeds: Ch 2, Ch 9 (Salts and the Precipitation Network), Ch 14 (Alkalis and Carbonates), Ch 36
