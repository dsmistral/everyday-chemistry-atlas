## N63 — The Home Scientist "021 - Calibrating a Disposable Pipette Gravimetrically" (1prcgMRG4Hk, 2010-03-31)
- Verification: watch page (6,982 views, Mar 31, 2010) + filmot auto-caption transcript. Transcript-level.
- Content: the quantitative-methodology face (the opener of a continuing series on basic laboratory techniques): "quantitative lab techniques depend on measuring masses and volumes accurately"; water's density is known very accurately -> the mass of 100 delivered drops gives the volume per drop -> accurate titrations [P9 instrumentless quantitation]. The balance recommendation: accurate to >= 0.01 g, capacity >= 100 g (200 better), inexpensive; the Jennings JS280 (~$35, Maker Shed and other vendors; excellent linearity/repeatability mid-range) [P1 BUY-branch apparatus sourcing + P10]. Procedure: tare the beaker (42.04 g), 10 drops = 0.37 g, consistent drop size (same pipette angle, let each drop form fully), 100 drops = 3.62 g. The calculation: water density at 20 C = 0.998 g/mL (CRC Handbook or Wikipedia); 3.62 g / 0.998 = 3.63 mL -> 0.0363 mL/drop -> inverted = 27.5 drops/mL [P9 + the temperature-dependent density datum]. THE EXTENSION — calibrate ANY glassware gravimetrically: a "100 mL" volumetric flask may actually hold 100.26 or 99.87 mL; use the ACTUAL volume, not the nominal, when computing solution concentrations [the calibration-correction methodology — the accuracy infrastructure for the P12 curriculum].
