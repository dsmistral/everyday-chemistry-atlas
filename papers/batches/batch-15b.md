## N68 — The Home Scientist "011 - Forensic Fingerprinting V: Acidified Peroxide" (6rvyetrDjJc, 2010-01-25)
- Verification: watch page (19,195 views, Jan 25, 2010) + filmot auto-caption transcript. Transcript-level.
- Content: the specialized BRASS face — acidified peroxide develops latent prints ONLY on bare brass surfaces (cartridge cases); the fifth of six fingerprinting segments. THE REALITY DATUM: latent prints are almost impossible to visualize on FIRED cartridge cases (extreme heat and pressure destroy the fragile prints) — the method's real use: a criminal who wipes a firearm clean but forgets the UNFIRED rounds in the magazine or cylinder [the honest-evidence-logic datum]. Sourcing: ONLY drugstore 3% hydrogen peroxide + distilled white vinegar (5% acetic acid) — the two-item drugstore method [P1]; the kit's brass cartridge case (FO-RKD) or any uncoated brass item. Quantitative prep: the mixture made FRESH immediately before use and the spent solution discarded afterwards (stability datum); the ratio "fairly important" — 10 parts H2O2 : 7 parts vinegar (he mixes 20 mL + 14 mL) [P9]. Process: immerse (it may float — hold it down); at first nothing happens, within 5-10 minutes the solution turns greenish near the case (dissolved copper — the color process cue); after ~15 minutes the surface is mottled; drain (discard down the drain — "a minor amount of copper... not enough to make any difference" — the disposal-honesty datum); rinse several times until the green tinge is gone; dry on a paper towel. Result: "very nice ridges" — a partial thumbprint + a partial fingerprint. THE COATED-BRASS CAVEAT: works only on UNCOATED brass — many cartridge cases and household brass objects are coated (the solution cannot reach the brass) -> fall back to superglue fuming (the N66 method-fallback chain) [P10].

## N69 — The Home Scientist "012 - Forensic Fingerprinting VI: Dusting" (BZNXFbG-ux4, 2010-01-27)
- Verification: watch page (125,453 views, Jan 27, 2010 — the highest-viewed episode of the channel) + filmot auto-caption transcript. Transcript-level.
- Content: the dusting face — the OLDEST technique of all, and the finale of the six-segment fingerprinting series. Method: a fine powder adheres to the skin oils present in the prints; excess removed by gently blowing or brushing; the prints photographed or lifted with transparent tape; used primarily on glass, metal, and other non-porous surfaces, particularly movable ones. THE HONEST-SKILL DATUM: "dusting is dangerous for the prints — although experienced technicians can work wonders, an inexperienced technician can destroy fragile latent prints faster by attempting to dust them than by any other method. I'm inexperienced but I'll give it my best shot" [the honest-experimenter face again]. THE SUBSTITUTION GEMS: commercial fingerprint powders come in many colors and varieties (magnetic, fluorescent dyes) but two suffice — a white powder for contrast on dark objects (he uses ordinary BABY POWDER) and a dark powder for light objects (make LAMPBLACK by smoking the bottom of a beaker with an ordinary candle) [P1/P10/P11]; the brush = a makeup brush, lens-cleaning brush, or even a fabric [P11]; the lift tape = special lift tape OR ordinary clear office/packing tape [P1]. Process control: dip the brush lightly, only a small amount on the tips of the bristles ("using too much powder is a real rookie mistake"); handle the specimen only where prints are not expected (his finger in the bottle mouth, thumb on the base); apply powder very lightly with a twisting motion; "you're much better off with too little powder than too much"; more prints are ruined by OVER-treatment than under-treatment [P9]; excess removed with a puffer bulb or canned air (nozzle at least 1 foot / 30 cm away, very short bursts — otherwise "you're liable to blow out the fingerprints as well as the excess powder") [P11]. Photograph BEFORE attempting the lift; the lift: smooth the tape gently with no wrinkles, pull off in one motion, transfer to a card contrasting in color with the powder [evidence-handling]. Reference: forensic lab session 8.1 (the Make: Science Room).
